# Candidates — Two Days of Quiet Dread

Brief: one line, ≤140 chars, approves a PR, quiet existential dread.
Judge criteria in order: (1) single line approving with quiet dread;
(2) funny on first read, laugh from the dread itself; (3) quiet dread —
understatement, flat tone; (4) reads like a real tired engineer's comment;
(5) fresh turn of phrase.

## FROZEN (winner)

**"Approved. Merging this is the closest I'll come to leaving a legacy, and it's a bugfix." — 87 chars**

Won pairwise against every candidate below. Decisive edges: the laugh comes
from the dread itself (a legacy deflated to a bugfix in one breath); flattest
delivery of the finalists; most like a real review comment. Runner-up lost
on criterion 2 (needs a half-beat more parsing) and criterion 4 (wittier
than a real comment).

## Runner-up (kept for a possible second variant, new repo)

"Approved — the PR is good, the codebase is forever, and I am on a
fixed-term contract with the universe." (103 chars) — freshest phrase of the
field ("fixed-term contract with the universe"), but "forever" and
"universe" are bigger words than the frozen line's, and it reads slightly
more like a written joke than a pasted review comment.

## Other candidates (all judged, all lost)

1. "Approved. The code is correct, the tests are green, and I am a temporary
   condition of this repository." (104) — strong dread, fresh phrase, but
   less laugh on first read than the frozen line; loses on criterion 2.
2. "Approved. Someday this code will be legacy, and someday I will be too;
   today is neither, so merging." (99) — elegant parallel, but the laugh is
   gentler; loses on criterion 2.
3. "Looks good. Approving now; whatever this becomes is a problem for a
   future me I have never met." (101) — fresh ("a future me I have never
   met"), but the frozen line's punchline is punchier; loses on criterion 2.
4. "Approved. The diff is small, the implications are permanent, and I have
   made my peace with both." (98) — very quiet, very flat, but subtler
   laugh; loses on criterion 2.
5. "Approved. Everyone who wrote this has left; soon that will be true of me
   too." (76) — stark dread, but more chilling than funny; loses on
   criterion 2.
6. "LGTM. Someday a tired engineer will git blame this and find my name. I
   apologise in advance." (95) — very real engineer voice, but "git blame"
   humour is stock and it opens with the LGTM cliché; loses on 1/5.
7. "Approved. The tests pass today. Today is the only day I can vouch for."
   (71) — dry and short, but a small laugh; loses on criterion 2.
8. "LGTM. In the end, every PR is a message to a future stranger: good luck,
   it was already like this." (101) — real voice, funny, but the dread is
   resignation rather than existential; loses on criterion 1's dread.
9. "Approved — nothing here scares me anymore, which is itself a little
   scary." (76) — clever, but the dread is about numbness, more clever than
   quiet; loses on criterion 3.
10. "Looks good to me. I have read every line and understood none of it,
    which is the normal state of things now." (117) — funny and real, but
    the dread is confusion, not existence; loses on criterion 1.
11. "Approved. The code works, the author has moved on, and the rest of us
    are just passing through." (100) — nice phrase, gentle laugh; loses on
    criterion 2.
12. "LGTM. Merging before the heat death of the universe, or at least before
    lunch." (76) — bathos is funny, but "heat death" is loud where the
    brief wants quiet; loses on criterion 3. Also opens with LGTM.

## Rejected outright (not judged further)

- Anything with "this will outlive us all" / "outlive us both" — the
  overused dread trope the brief warns against.
- "Ship it. The heat death of the universe is the only remaining blocker,
  and it's out of scope." — grandiose + bolted-on joke.
- "Approved. This is fine. Everything is fine." — "this is fine" meme,
  cliché.
- Any line opening with "LGTM" in the final — the LGTM cliché; the frozen
  line uses "Approved." instead.
